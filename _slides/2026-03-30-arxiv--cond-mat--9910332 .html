But the problem is connections are not random; real networks have hubs, follow a power law, and are usually not static. --- ### Key Empirical Observation By studying some real world networks (e.g. the Web, movie-actor, scientific citations, electrical powergrid of western US), the authors noticed a pattern. The probability that a vertex in a network interacts with $k$ other vertices follows: $$ P(k) \\thicksim k^{-\\gamma} $$ with $\\gamma \\in [2, 4]$ This is independent of the system, it's a .nord14[scale-free network] --- ### The Problem with ER and WS - Both models assume we start with a fixed number of edges - The degrees of ER follows a poisson law - This leads to the 2 principal mechanism proposed by the paper --- ### Core Idea - .nord14[Continuous Growth] : start with a number $m_0$ of nodes and add new ones over time - .nord14[Preferential Attachment] : those new nodes connect to already well-connected nodes $$ \\Pi(k_i) = \\frac{k_i}{\\sum_j k_j} $$ --- ### The BA Model 1. Start with a small network of $m_0$ nodes 2. At each time step: * Add a new node * Connect it to $m$ existing nodes with probability $\\Pi$ this gives a model with $\\gamma_{model} = 2.9 \\pm 0.1$ --- ### Validation - A model without preferential attachment was tested and does not produce the scale-free feature - A model with fixed number of vertices was tested and it leads to fully connected network --- ### Conclusion - Real networks are not random - Birth of scale-free networks - Early nodes accumulate links and become hubs
