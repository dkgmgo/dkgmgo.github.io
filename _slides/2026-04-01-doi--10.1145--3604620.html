Large cloud providers like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft operate massive, global private network infrastructures - Increasingly, Internet traffic bypasses the public Internet and rides their private fiber entirely --- ### \`traceroute\` is Dead - Historically, researchers used \`traceroute\` to map internet paths - Today, the tool is increasingly unreliable because: - Hyperscalers can block probes or modify packets - Middleboxes (NAT, DPI) distort measurements - Routers may respond from respond from random interfaces --- ### The Problem with today's Internet > Hyperscalers now carry most internet traffic but we have largely lost the ability to measure their networks --- ### The Solution #### Lightweight measurements - .nord14[The round-trip time (RTT)] is difficult to hide and it can be inferred from the TCP handshake - .nord14[The geo-location] of infrastructures can be approximated #### Heavyweight math: Assume the data has been generated from an .nord14[unknown complex topological space] and try to .nord14[learn] it (a continuous manifold) --- class: nord-light, center, middle # The Paper's Methodology .letter-spacing-20[The five-step pipeline] --- ### Step 1: Measurement and Data Reduction .column-2.font-xs[ - Measure all-pairs latency among a set of nodes and compute the residual latencies matrix $$ RL = \\frac{RTT}{2} - \\frac{GCD}{\\frac{2c}{3}} $$ * RL: the residual latency * RTT: the measured round-trip time * GCD: [the great circle distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great-circle_distance) * $\\frac{2c}{3}$: the speed of light in fiber - A performance threshold $\\epsilon$ defines "nearly straight" edges (latency close to the physical optimum) .center[ ![](/assets/doi--10.1145--3604620/step-1.png) ] ] --- ### Step 2: Graph Construction For a given $\\epsilon$ edges with smaller residual latencies form a graph small $\\epsilon$ $\\implies$ local structure large $\\epsilon$ $\\implies$ global structure .center[ ![](/assets/doi--10.1145--3604620/step-2.png) ] --- ### Step 3: Ricci Curvature .column-2.font-xs[ - Calculate the .nord14[Ollivier-Ricci curvature] of each edge to assess their importance - The curvature is used because it measures the optimal transport of mass between neighborhoods of nodes - Since each $\\epsilon$ defines a graph topology, it's possible to visualize them all on a boxplot ##### Intuition - negative curvature $\\implies$ bridge/bottleneck - zero curvature $\\implies$ grid-like - positive curvature $\\implies$ dense/redundant .center[ ![](/assets/doi--10.1145--3604620/step-3.png) ] ] --- ### Step 4: Characterization of edge behavior To characterize the contribution of an edge the authors have 2 metrics: - The .nord14[performance] (right heatmap): it's the edge's residual latency i.e. the value of $\\epsilon$ at which the edge emerges - The .nord14[importance] (left heatmap): it's the impact of the edge on network robustness (edges with lower curvature are more important) .center[ ] --- ### Step 5: Characterization of node behavior - A [Sankey diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankey_diagram) is used to visualize the evolution of the topology as $\\epsilon$ increases. - It also shows the well performing clusters and how they are created .center[ ] --- class: nord-light, center, middle # The Results .letter-spacing-20[The Internet is no longer a graph, it’s a curved space] --- ### The manifold view - Peak $\\implies$ strong local connectivity - Saddle $\\implies$ critical bridge - Flat $\\implies$ neutral structure .center[ ] .float-right.width-10.center[ ![](/assets/doi--10.1145--3604620/peak.png) ] --- ### Key Findings - The authors applied the methodology on GC, AWS and Azure. They present results for all providers and analyze Azure in detail - The 3 hyperscalers each show 3 well-performing regions (North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific) and other regions with significantly poorer performance, such as Africa - On the Azure network examples of critical links include Tokyo - Quincy and Mumbai - London - Africa and South America are weakly connected to the rest of the network - The results show that geography and geopolitics strongly influence network structure --- .center[ ] --- ### Conclusion - From discrete (graph theory) to continuous (differential geometry) - From Observation to Inference: "We can't see the network so we infer it" - Geometry (curvature) as a big source of information
